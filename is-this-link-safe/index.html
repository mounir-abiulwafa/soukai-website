Link safety

Is this link safe? Look for context before you click.

A URL can look convincing even when the message around it is suspicious. Treat an unexpected link as a request to verify—not as proof that the sender is legitimate.

Warning signs in a link

  • A close-but-not-exact spelling of a brand or website name.
  • Extra words, unusual subdomains, shortened links, or a destination that does not match the message.
  • A link delivered with an urgent request to sign in, pay, or verify a code.
  • A message that makes sense only if you click immediately.

Check without clicking

  1. Do not open the URL from the message while you are uncertain.
  2. Look at the full address, if your device shows it, and compare it with the official domain you know.
  3. Open the organization’s official app or type its known website address yourself.
  4. Use an independent contact method to confirm a supposed alert or request.
Fictional example“Bank security: verify now at secure-examplebank-login.com.” The name may resemble a real bank, but an unexpected login demand and an unfamiliar domain are reasons to use the bank’s official app instead.

If you already opened the link

Closing a page may be enough if you did not enter information or download anything. If you entered a password, change it from the real service’s website and review account activity. If you entered payment information, contact your bank or card provider right away.

How the app approaches links
