How to Check a Scam Screenshot With AI

Take a screenshot of a suspicious message, email, link, or payment request and get an AI-assisted risk assessment before you click, reply, or pay.

Use Is This a Scam?See how it works

Quick answer

You can use a scam screenshot checker when suspicious content cannot be copied easily or when the visual context matters. Add the screenshot to Is This a Scam? AI to receive an AI-assisted risk assessment, a risk level, reasons that may indicate concern, and a recommended next action. It is a second opinion, not a guarantee that the screenshot is safe or fraudulent.

What can you check from a screenshot?

SMS & messages

Text-message and messaging-app screenshots, including suspicious links or payment requests.

Emails & alerts

Suspicious email screenshots, account-security warnings, and fake bank alerts.

Offers & payments

Delivery notices, job offers, marketplace requests, social DMs, and payment prompts.

How it works

  1. Take a screenshot. Save the suspicious message, email, social DM, delivery notice, or payment request.
  2. Add it to the app. Choose the screenshot you want Is This a Scam? AI to analyze.
  3. Let AI assess risk signals. The app analyzes the screenshot and may identify suspicious patterns such as urgency, impersonation, or payment pressure.
  4. Review the result. See the risk level, reasons, and recommended next action, then verify important matters independently.
Fictional example A screenshot shows a delivery message: “Your package is on hold. Pay $1.40 at delivery-payment-check.example today.” This is fictional. A small payment request, urgency, and an unfamiliar domain are signals to pause rather than use the link.

Red flags a screenshot check may help surface

  • Urgency, threats, or claims that an account or package problem must be fixed immediately.
  • Payment requests, gift-card instructions, or pressure to send money outside a normal process.
  • Suspicious links, unfamiliar domains, or a login page that does not match the real service.
  • Impersonation of a delivery service, bank, employer, marketplace, or support team.
  • Requests for passwords, verification codes, full card details, or unusual contact methods.
  • Social-media or messaging conversations that turn quickly toward money, private details, or an off-platform move.

What to do before you act

Do not click links from the screenshot or message. Do not send passwords, one-time verification codes, or full card details. Instead, open the real service’s app or type its known address into a new browser window, then check orders, account alerts, and support options from there. For a suspicious URL, see how to check a suspicious link before you continue.

What if you already clicked or paid?

Close the page and do not download files or install software it suggests. If you entered credentials, change the password through the real account and anywhere it was reused. If you entered card or payment details, contact the payment provider promptly through a trusted official channel. If you sent money or a verification code, contact the relevant provider as soon as possible and be wary of follow-up “recovery” messages.

Why use a screenshot instead of copying the text?

A screenshot can be useful when a message cannot be copied, when the suspicious content is inside a social app or email client, or when layout, sender details, payment prompts, and surrounding context matter. It can give you a fast second opinion without asking you to recreate the message manually.

Scam screenshot checker FAQ

Can AI tell if a screenshot is a scam?

AI can assess risk signals in a screenshot, but it cannot guarantee that content is safe or fraudulent. Verify important issues through official channels.

Can I check a text-message or WhatsApp screenshot?

Yes. You can add a suspicious screenshot to Is This a Scam? AI for an AI-assisted risk assessment of supported screenshot content.

Can I check a suspicious email screenshot?

Yes. A screenshot can preserve visual context when copying an email is inconvenient. Still verify important account or payment issues independently.

Is it safe to upload a screenshot?

Before uploading, remove information you do not need to share, such as passwords, verification codes, full card numbers, or other sensitive details.

What should I do if I already clicked the link?

Close the page, avoid entering more information, and use the real service’s official app or site to secure any affected account or payment method.

Can the app guarantee a screenshot is fraudulent?

No. Is This a Scam? AI provides an AI-assisted second opinion, not a guarantee. Use it alongside independent verification.
