Package Delivery Fee Text: Scam or Real?

A package on-hold or redelivery-fee text can look routine, especially when the amount is small. Treat it as unverified until you check the delivery through an official service or seller channel you open yourself.

Quick answer

A package delivery fee scam may say your parcel is on hold, an address needs confirmation, or a small customs or redelivery fee is due. Do not use the text link to verify it. Open the delivery service’s official app or site yourself, or check the original seller’s order page.

Why a small delivery fee can be risky

A fake delivery-fee request can use a small amount to feel ordinary and urgent. The goal may be card details, personal information, or access to a fake payment page. No single phrase proves a message is fraudulent, but a fee request combined with pressure, an unfamiliar delivery domain, or a request for sensitive details may indicate risk.

Fictional example “Delivery update: Your package is on hold. Confirm your address and pay a $1.25 fee at parcel-fee-review.example before midnight.” This is fictional. The deadline, small charge, and unfamiliar domain are reasons to verify through a known official delivery channel instead.

Red flags in a delivery fee text

  • A request for a small redelivery, customs, address-confirmation, or release fee.
  • Urgency such as “expires today,” “final notice,” or “package will be returned.”
  • A delivery or payment domain you do not recognize, including a brand name combined with extra words.
  • A request for card details, account credentials, a one-time code, or more personal information than a delivery update should need.
  • A package reference that does not match an order you can find independently.
  • A request to call or reply to a number in the suspicious message.

How to verify a delivery fee independently

  1. Do not tap the payment link, reply, or call the number in the message.
  2. Open the delivery service’s official app or type its known website address into a new browser window.
  3. Use tracking details from your original order confirmation, not a number supplied only by the text.
  4. Check the seller’s order page or contact the seller through a trusted support route.
  5. If a fee is genuinely required, confirm it in that official channel before entering any payment details.

If you already clicked or submitted information

Close the page without downloading anything further. If you entered a password, change it on the real service and anywhere it was reused. If you entered card or payment information, contact the card issuer or payment provider promptly through a trusted number or app. If you submitted personal information, be cautious of follow-up texts that use it to sound convincing and verify any later request independently.

Package delivery fee scam FAQ

Is a delivery fee text always a scam?

No. The safer approach is to verify the delivery through an official channel you open yourself instead of relying on the text link.

Can a fake package fee page collect my card details?

It may be designed to do that. Contact the payment provider promptly if you entered details and follow its guidance.

What if I did not order anything?

Do not use the message link to investigate. Ignore or report the text as appropriate, and check only orders you can identify through trusted services.
