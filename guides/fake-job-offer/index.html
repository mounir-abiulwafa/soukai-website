Fake job offer: warning signs and safe next steps.

A real opportunity should stand up to careful questions. Be cautious when a recruiter rushes you, asks you to pay, or wants sensitive details before a normal hiring process.

Fake job offer red flags

  • A job is offered immediately without a credible interview or clear role details.
  • It promises unusually high pay for little work or asks you to act before you can research the company.
  • You are asked to pay for equipment, training, a background check, or a “starter kit.”
  • The recruiter asks for identity, banking, or login details earlier than a legitimate process requires.

How to check an offer

  1. Search for the company independently and use its official careers page.
  2. Compare the recruiter’s email domain with the company’s known domain.
  3. Ask for a written job description, interview details, and a way to verify the recruiter.
  4. Do not pay to receive a job or deposit a check on someone else’s behalf.
Fictional example“You are hired today. Send your ID and $75 for a laptop deposit before the interview.” A fee before a verified hiring process is a strong reason to pause and check the company through its official website.

If you already sent information or money

Stop communicating through the suspicious contact. Contact your bank or payment provider if money was sent. If you shared an ID, password, or account details, ask the relevant provider about securing the account and consider identity-protection steps appropriate where you live.
