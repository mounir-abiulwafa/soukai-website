Is This QR Code Safe? How to Spot a QR Code Scam

A QR code can hide its destination until you scan it. That does not make every code unsafe, but it does mean you should inspect the destination and avoid entering information or paying until you can verify the service independently.

Quick answer

A QR code scam, sometimes called QR phishing or quishing, may send you to a fake login or payment page. After scanning, inspect the displayed destination before opening it fully or entering anything. If the code appears on an unexpected email, text, sticker, restaurant table, or parking meter, use the official service’s app or website instead when you can.

How QR phishing can work

A QR code can make a destination harder to judge at first glance. Scammers may place a fake QR sticker over a legitimate one, include a code in an unexpected message, or use a code that leads to a page designed to collect a password, payment details, or a verification code. The code itself does not prove intent; the destination and the request are what need scrutiny.

Fictional example A parking sign has a QR sticker that opens pay-parking-now.example and immediately asks for card details. This is fictional. A new sticker, an unfamiliar payment domain, or pressure to pay before you can compare it with the official parking service may indicate risk.

QR code scam warning signs

  • A QR code arrives in an unexpected email or text that claims you must act quickly.
  • A sticker appears to cover another code on a parking meter, poster, menu, or payment terminal.
  • The page asks for an account login, one-time code, or payment details without a clear reason.
  • The destination uses a lookalike name, misspelling, odd subdomain, or unrelated domain.
  • The page creates urgency with an expiring payment, account lock, or limited-time claim.
  • You cannot independently match the destination to the business or service you intended to use.

How to inspect a QR destination safely

  1. Use your phone’s preview of the destination, if available, before opening it.
  2. Read the domain carefully and do not rely on a familiar word inside a longer address.
  3. For parking, restaurant, account, or payment services, open the official app or type the known website yourself.
  4. Do not enter credentials, a code, or card details just because the QR page asks for them.
  5. If you receive the QR code in a message, verify the request through a separate trusted channel.

If you already scanned a QR code

If you only scanned it and did not submit information, close the page and navigate independently to the service you meant to use. If you entered a password, change it in the real account and anywhere it was reused. If you entered card or payment details, contact the payment provider promptly through a trusted channel. A screenshot of the code, the resulting URL, or the message can be useful context for a second opinion.

QR code scam FAQ

Can I tell whether a QR code is safe by looking at it?

Not reliably. Inspect the destination shown by your device and compare it with an official service before you continue.

Should I use a QR code to pay for parking?

Use the official parking app or site when available. If a QR sticker seems altered or the destination is unfamiliar, do not enter payment information.

What if a QR code opened a login page?

Close it and open the official app or website yourself. If you already entered credentials, change the password through the real service promptly.
